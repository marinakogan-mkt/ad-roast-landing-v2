What the teardown found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

The ad copy names Mythos and runtime enforcement in the same beat. That is a specific, category-aware frame the buyer recognizes instantly, and it is the thing that earns the click.

What’s happening

“Attacks stopped. Identities protected.” and “the most advanced AI meets the strongest identity defense” could belong to any identity vendor. The image leads with drama instead of the Mythos and runtime-enforcement frame the body just earned attention with.

The fix

Put the specific frame on the image too: name runtime enforcement and what it stops, so the strongest message is the first thing the eye lands on.

What’s happening

The landing page has a good H1 and a concrete sub-pillar (service accounts, NTLM, legacy apps, OT). FAQ #1 is the best asset on the page, because it answers the buyer’s real fear about rip-and-replace.

What’s happening

The strongest message is buried below the fold, while an anonymous testimonial up top raises over-blocking fear instead of reducing it. The bottom of the page then widens into eight sub-products and dilutes the runtime-control story.

The fix

Move the rip-and-replace answer above the fold, drop the anonymous testimonial, and add the real homepage logo strip (GM, Singtel, Rio Tinto) so the proof shows up before the buyer has to scroll.

What the roast scored · tap a row

“When the most advanced AI meets the strongest identity defense” sounds like a movie trailer, not a security claim a CISO can evaluate or forward internally.
“Learn more” button undersells a demo-intent offer and mismatches the sales-led ask; no urgency or specificity about what happens after the click.
Generic humanoid robot glowing with particles reads as stock sci-fi, not identity security; nothing in the visual depicts runtime controls, access, or attack chains.
“Attacks stopped. Identities protected.” is a slogan, not a benefit; no mention of runtime enforcement, Mythos specifics, or measurable outcome shown in the body text.
No named customers, no proof points, no callout extension with a stat or certification anywhere in the ad text or creative.

Questions, answered

The ad body does one genuinely smart thing: it names Mythos and runtime enforcement in the same beat, a frame a CISO recognizes instantly. The problem is sequencing, not quality. The image then trades that specificity for vague superlatives, and the landing page keeps its most convincing copy farthest from first read.
The strongest message shows up last. The ad image leads with category language instead of the Mythos and runtime frame that earned the click. On the page, the best asset, the FAQ that answers the rip-and-replace fear, sits below the fold, while an anonymous testimonial raises over-blocking fear up top.
Lead with the strongest message, everywhere. Put the Mythos and runtime-enforcement frame on the image, move the rip-and-replace answer above the fold, drop the anonymous testimonial, and add the real logo strip (GM, Singtel, Rio Tinto) so the proof shows up before the buyer scrolls.
