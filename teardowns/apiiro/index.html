What the roast found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

“Shift Left Is Dead,” “Continuous Threat Modeling,” “Guardian Agent”: three brand layers that stop an AppSec scroll and signal a point of view. The attention is earned.

What’s happening

The hook names three big ideas with no numbers, no proof and no product shown. The Guardian Agent is named four times and appears only as a cartoon mascot, never as the working product, so the single strongest capability stays invisible.

The fix

Show the Guardian Agent doing one concrete thing, and attach one number, so the provocation has something to land on.

What’s happening

The destination is a 1,000-word CEO blog post with the product buried in paragraph seven. A reader who clicked a punchy three-word hook has to read an essay to find out what Apiiro actually does.

The fix

Send the click to the Guardian Agent itself, a page that shows the capability the ad kept naming, not a thought-leadership post.

Why it scores 5/10 · tap a row

“Shift Left Is Dead” grabs attention and the ad body explains why with specifics like code-to-runtime, but the creative headline alone is vague without the body copy.
“Learn more” is fine for content offers, but it drops AppSec leaders into a 1,000-word CEO essay instead of the Guardian Agent itself, killing any goodwill the copy built.
Cute robot mascot (Guardian Agent) undercuts the serious, technical tone of the copy talking about architecture and prompt-triggered risk analysis.
Concrete mechanics cited: “Runs on real architecture (code to runtime)”, “Triggers on every design, prompt, or change” beats generic AI security fluff.
No named customers, no proof numbers, no callout extensions in the ad text itself; claims like “real prevention” are asserted, not backed.

Questions, answered

The hook is genuinely sharp, three brand layers that earn an AppSec scroll. It scores 5 because the ad names big ideas without a single number, proof point or product shot, and the strongest capability it keeps naming, the Guardian Agent, appears only as a cartoon mascot.
At the click. A punchy, provocative ad lands on a 1,000-word CEO blog post with the product buried in paragraph seven. The energy of the hook dissolves into an essay before the reader finds out what Apiiro does.
Show, then send. Put the Guardian Agent and one real number in the ad, and point the click at a page that demonstrates that capability, not a founder essay. The hook is strong enough to carry a concrete payoff.
If your ad names a capability, ask: do I show it, and does the click land on it? A sharp hook that never shows the product, pointed at a blog post, spends attention it cannot cash. AdRoast scores this automatically, and the first roast is free.
