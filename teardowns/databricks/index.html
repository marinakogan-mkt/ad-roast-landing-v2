What the roast found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

“How Databricks manages AI coding costs” is a clear, specific promise, and the post body backs it.

What’s happening

The infographic quantifies each lever and repeats the ad copy’s four techniques almost word for word.

What’s happening

Figures like “~50%+ savings” give real specificity, but they are labeled as directional estimates, which softens the claim a little.

The fix

Pair one estimate with one measured result from the Databricks team.

What’s happening

“Managing AI Coding Costs at Scale” matches the ad, and the page repeats the same four levers. There is no bait and switch.

What’s happening

Patrick Wendell’s byline in the ad, and Stripe, Coinbase, Uber and Ramp on the page, give the argument real peer credibility.

What’s happening

The site menu is there, but inside the article there is no link to the product, no email capture and no prompt to talk to anyone.

The fix

Add one next step that continues the playbook, like the product behind the routing lever.

Why it scores 7/10 · tap a row

’How Databricks manages AI coding costs’ on the creative is a clear, specific promise matched by the post body. Does exactly what it says.
’See the full playbook’ is soft and fits an open-content ask, no friction, though the creative itself shows no visible button text.
Infographic quantifies each lever (50%, 30%, 10%, 10% savings), reinforcing the ad copy’s four techniques almost word for word.
Numbers like ’~50%+ savings’ give real specificity, though they’re labeled directional estimates, not audited results, slightly softening the claim.
A named exec (Patrick Wendell, VP Engineering) and the Databricks brand itself carry credibility; image-based badges are valid on LinkedIn but absent here.

Questions, answered

The ad, the infographic and the blog tell the same story with the same numbers, so the reader never has to reorient after the click.
A next step inside the article. The playbook ends with no product link, no email capture and no prompt to talk to anyone.
Put the argument in the creative, repeat it in the same words on the page, and name real people and peers behind it.
