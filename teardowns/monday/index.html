What’s happening

“Spot programme risk before leadership asks” names a precise fear a PMO leader feels instantly. That specificity is what earns the click on a cold LinkedIn feed.

The fix

Keep the hook. Add one proof point so the claim is not pure assertion.

What’s happening

A stressed person clutching their head shows the failure state. It dramatizes the problem but never shows monday.com actually spotting the risk early, so the visual undercuts the product promise.

The fix

Show the product catching the risk, the save, not the panic.

What’s happening

No stat, no logo, no named customer, even though LinkedIn supports image-based trust signals. A department head scrolling past has no reason to believe the claim.

The fix

Add a number or a recognizable customer logo to the creative itself.

What’s happening

The click hits the broad monday.com brand page, not a page built around the programme-risk angle the ad used. The buyer who clicked for that specific pain has to go re-find it.

The fix

Route this ad to a page that opens with the same PMO / risk problem the ad names.

What’s happening

The ad’s CTA promises “Book a demo,” a sales-led next step. The page funnels into a no-credit-card self-serve signup instead. The intent the ad set up does not match the action the page offers.

The fix

Align the next step: if the ad earns a demo, the page should book one, not swap it for self-serve.

What’s happening

A sales-led ad into a self-serve page splits the visitor’s intent. The high-intent click the ad worked to earn leaks between the two, which is where the demos quietly disappear.

The fix

One ad, one promise, one matching next step carried onto the page.

Why it scores 6/10

“Spot programme risk before leadership asks” is a clear, specific fear a department head recognizes instantly, a solid hook for cold LinkedIn traffic.
The image, a stressed person clutching their head, sells panic, not the product spotting risk early. It shows the failure state, not the save the ad is selling.
“Spot risk before leadership asks” is vague on the mechanism. It never names the dashboards, automations, or AI agents that would actually power the claim.
The creative says “Book a demo,” but the page funnels to a no-credit-card free signup, not a sales call. The next step the ad promised is not the one the page delivers.
No stat, logo, or customer name in the ad itself, even though LinkedIn supports image-based trust signals. The claim stands on assertion alone.

Questions, answered

The creative is strong, the sharpest ads name a precise role pain (a PMO spotting programme risk before leadership asks). The weak point is the handoff: the ad promises “Book a demo,” but the click funnels into a no-credit-card free signup, so the intent the ad set up gets dropped.
The ad invites you to book a demo, a guided walkthrough with their team. The click drops you into a free signup to set it up yourself instead. That is not automatically bad, plenty of people happily self-serve, but someone who was ready for a demo now has to switch gears, and those tend to be the most sales-ready visitors.
