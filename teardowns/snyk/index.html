The ad promised a six-step guide.
The click got a homepage.

The ad picks a timely pain, developers shipping AI-generated code faster than security can review it, and offers a sensible next step: a six-step guide. Then the funnel breaks. The click lands on Snyk’s general homepage, with no reference to the guide, and the visitor is asked to sign up, explore the platform or book a demo. That pause to reorient is where qualified traffic leaks out.

What the roast found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

“Developers are shipping AI-generated code faster than security teams can review it” is a real, current problem. A six-step guide is the right size of ask for that audience and that stage.

What’s happening

The creative leads with a polished title about stabilizing, optimizing and scaling. The idea buyers care about first, that security cannot keep up with development speed, sits lower down.

The fix

Lead with the pain in the headline and let the guide title support it.

What’s happening

The copy talks about visibility and governance that scales. It never says what the reader gets: less manual review, vulnerable AI-written code caught earlier, or more output governed without adding headcount.

The fix

Make the guide outcome specific, so the download has a stated payoff.

What’s happening

The ad promises a specific whitepaper, a simple download and six practical steps. The homepage headline talks about unleashing AI innovators securely, with no mention of the six-step framework or AI code governance.

The fix

Send the traffic to a dedicated page built around that exact asset.

What’s happening

The visitor came for a guide and now has to choose between signing up, exploring the platform and booking a live demo. That is a different offer with a higher commitment level.

The fix

Use one CTA from ad to page: Download the 6-Step Guide. Earn the next step after the guide has delivered value.

Why it scores 6/10 · tap a row

Creative headline ’Stabilize, Optimize, Scale: A Prescriptive Path for AI Security’ is jargon-forward; the intro line about developers outpacing security review is clearer and should lead.
’Download Guide’ on the creative and ’Learn more’ in copy are low friction and appropriate for top-of-funnel content, though the two CTAs slightly compete.
Creative promises ’6 steps’ toward governance, matching the ad body copy, but the whitepaper title itself doesn’t mention the number 6 or governance explicitly.
’Foundational visibility to automated governance that scales’ is vague; no concrete outcome like reduced review time or vulnerability counts is cited.
No named customers, ratings, or numeric proof appear in the ad text or callouts; Snyk’s strong stats (288% ROI, 80% faster scans) live only on the LP, unused in the ad.

Questions, answered

The ad itself is reasonable. The break is after the click: it offers a six-step AI code governance guide and lands on Snyk’s general homepage, which never mentions the guide.
No. When someone clicks for a specific asset, the page should give them that asset. A homepage makes them sort through the whole company to find it, and asks for a bigger commitment than the one they agreed to.
Lead the ad with the pain, name the outcome of the guide, send the click to a dedicated page for that asset, keep one CTA throughout, and bring one relevant proof point into the ad or the page.
