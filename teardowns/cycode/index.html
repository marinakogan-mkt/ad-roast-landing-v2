What the roast found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

Gartner, IDC, GigaOm and Frost & Sullivan are named in the copy and shown as logos on the creative. That is strong, specific, third-party validation, and it is rare for a LinkedIn ad. It is exactly what a consolidation-minded security buyer wants to see.

What’s happening

“The Leader Four Analysts Agree On” reads like a garbled Yoda quote. The layout is clean, but the syntax makes you reread it, and it never says leader in what.

The fix

Say it straight: name the category the four analysts rank Cycode in, so the proof lands on the first read.

What’s happening

“Cannot keep pace with the risk agentic development creates” names the threat but never says what Cycode actually stops, or how fast. The buyer gets the fear without the fix.

The fix

Add one concrete outcome: what Cycode catches that the buyer’s current stack misses.

What’s happening

The page opens on “#1 AI Native Agentic Development Security Platform.” The four analyst badges that sold the click are gone above the fold, replaced by internal language (ADLC, Software Supply Chain Security). Real logos are there (UBS, Broadcom, Nutanix), but they are disconnected from the ad’s promise.

The fix

Lead the page with the same four analysts the ad used. Keep the argument that earned the click alive above the fold.

Questions, answered

The ad is strong where it counts: four independent analyst firms, named in copy and shown as logos, is credible third-party proof aimed straight at the CISO and AppSec buyer. The weak points are a headline you have to read twice and a benefit that names the threat without naming the save.
After the click. The ad earns attention with analyst validation, then the landing page drops those badges above the fold and swaps the argument for internal jargon like ADLC and Software Supply Chain Security. The message that won the click is replaced by the one the company wants to explain.
Carry the proof through. Lead the landing page with the same four analysts the ad used, keep that argument above the fold, and add one concrete outcome to the ad so the benefit is a save, not just a threat.
