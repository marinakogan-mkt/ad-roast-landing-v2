Two hooks in one ad.
The page picks the right one.

Hyperproof turned a 15-question maturity quiz into a game, and that is a real offer. “CISO for a Day” is a stronger idea than almost anything else in GRC advertising. The ad just does not commit to it: the creative shouts “The Proof Is In the Platform,” the headline says something else, and the button under both says “Learn more.” The page knows what it is, and it is the best part of this funnel.

What the roast found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

The creative says “The Proof Is In the Platform.” The headline says “CISO for a Day: Play the Scenarios, Get Your Score.” The reader has to decide which one the ad is about.

The fix

Lead the creative with “CISO for a Day.” It is the offer, and the slogan is not.

What’s happening

“9 real scenarios, one GRC maturity score” tells the reader the size of the commitment and exactly what they get at the end. Most compliance ads never get this concrete.

What’s happening

The creative’s own button says “Play CISO for a Day.” LinkedIn’s button says “Learn more.” Nobody learns more from a game, they play it.

The fix

Match the written call to action to the creative’s button.

What’s happening

There is no customer name, logo or number on the creative. The page has an Acuity International story, and the ad doesn’t use it.

The fix

Add the Acuity result as one line on the creative.

What’s happening

“Your board wants answers, your auditor wants evidence, and your third-party vendor list just doubled” is the ICP’s week, written in the game’s own voice.

What’s happening

The button stays inside the role-play, where a generic “Start now” would break it. It is a small choice that makes the whole page feel designed.

What’s happening

Acuity International and its “1/3 of the time” result sit far below the first call to action. It is the only proof on the page.

The fix

Move the Acuity number up beside “Start my shift” and add two or three logos.

Why it scores 7/10 · tap a row

Creative says ’The Proof Is In the Platform’ but ad copy headline is ’CISO for a Day: Play the Scenarios, Get Your Score.’ Two different hooks competing for attention.
’Learn more’ is soft for an interactive game offer. Creative’s own button says ’Play CISO for a Day,’ which is punchier and matches intent better.
Creative headline ’The Proof Is In the Platform’ never appears in ad copy text, and copy’s ’no 15-question quiz required’ isn’t echoed on the creative at all.
’9 real scenarios, one GRC maturity score’ is concrete and specific, a real step up from vague ’improve your compliance posture’ filler.
No customer name, logo, or proof point in the ad itself, LinkedIn supports it but this creative skips it entirely.

Questions, answered

It has a great offer and does not lead with it. The creative’s slogan and the “CISO for a Day” headline compete, and “Learn more” undersells an interactive game.
It stays in character. The copy speaks to the buyer’s real week, “Start my shift” keeps the role-play going, and the “What you get” list says exactly what comes out of the game.
It answers from decisions rather than self-reported ratings, and that makes the score more believable. The ad should say so, because “no 15-question quiz required” is a real difference.
