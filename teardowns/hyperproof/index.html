What the teardown found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

“You approved the vendor. Who approved their sub-processors?” is clear in under 12 words and gives a privacy team a concrete reason to click. It speaks to one buyer, the privacy lead, and does not try to speak to anyone else.

What’s happening

Strong structure, credible customer logos (Reddit, Nutanix, Fortinet, Appian), a genuinely low-friction four-field form, no credit card and no vendor logins. As a page, it has real assets.

What’s happening

The page switches to a broad TPRM and GRC story for CISOs and audit. “Sub-processor” and “personal data,” the exact words that made the privacy lead click, are gone. A precise privacy click lands on a generic compliance page.

The fix

Build a privacy-lead version of the page, or at least a section above the fold that keeps the sub-processor and personal-data language the ad used, so the buyer sees their own problem continue.

Questions, answered

Yes, the hook is one of the sharpest in the series: under 12 words, aimed at exactly one buyer, the DPO or privacy lead, with a concrete reason to click. It scores 6 overall because the funnel loses that focus after the click, not because the ad is weak.
On the landing page. The ad speaks to a privacy lead about sub-processors and personal data; the page switches to a broad GRC and TPRM story for CISOs, compliance and audit, and the specific privacy language disappears. The page has good assets, just aimed at a different buyer.
Keep the buyer the ad chose. Carry the sub-processor and personal-data language onto the page, at least above the fold, so the privacy lead sees their own problem continue instead of landing on a generic compliance pitch.
