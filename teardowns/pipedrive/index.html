Pipedrive’s ad opens with a quote from George Vlasyev about the scramble after a sales call to remember what mattered. It hints at notes, or AI recall. But the video thumbnail is just the Pipedrive logo on green, the name carries no title, no feature or number is offered, and the button says Sign Up with nothing explaining what for. The homepage it reaches is solid. The ad never earns the click that gets there.

What the roast found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

The copy implies meeting notes or AI recall after a call, but the video thumbnail is just the Pipedrive logo on a green background. Zero visual support for the idea being sold.

The fix

Show the payoff: a deal record filling in with call notes, or the AI summary the quote hints at. Let the creative carry the promise.

What’s happening

George Vlasyev is cited as the source, with no title, company, or role. On a cold LinkedIn feed a bare name adds no authority, so the quote reads as filler.

The fix

Give the name a reason to matter, a title and company, or drop it and lead with the pain itself.

What’s happening

“Walk away knowing what mattered” is a mood, not an outcome a sales manager can act on. No feature, no number, no concrete result tied to Pipedrive.

The fix

Name what Pipedrive does: every call logged to the deal automatically, so nothing from the conversation gets lost.

What’s happening

Sign Up is the right button for a free-trial product, but nothing in the copy or the creative says what you are signing up for or why now. The click has to be taken on faith.

The fix

Tie the CTA to the payoff, for example: Start free, keep every call on the record.

What’s happening

The page the ad reaches, the easy and effective CRM for closing deals, is clear and on-message for the sales buyer. The gap is upstream, not here.

The fix

Keep the page. Rebuild the ad so the promise it makes matches what the homepage pays off.
