What the roast found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

“Hardcoded Secrets. Overprivileged Agents. Suspicious Skills.” is specific language this buyer uses every day. It frames the threat before it sells anything.

What’s happening

The body leads with overprivileged agents and the creative leads with hardcoded secrets and suspicious skills. The two run in parallel instead of building on each other.

The fix

Pick one threat and carry it through the copy, the creative and the page.

What’s happening

No named customer, no logo, no analyst mention and no number. A free assessment still asks a security team to let a new vendor in.

The fix

Add one named customer or one finding from a real assessment.

What’s happening

The ad promises a free 90-day assessment. The page says “Book a demo” everywhere, which is a different ask with more friction.

The fix

Make the assessment the page’s only CTA, in the same words as the ad.

What’s happening

“Smart AI Governance. Zero Friction.” is category language. It leaves behind the breach fear that earned the click, and the tone moves to “Marie Kondo for your AI jungle.”

The fix

Open with “Overprivileged Agents Are Your Next Breach,” then show how the assessment finds them.

What’s happening

Zero customer logos, case studies, certifications or named enterprise users, in front of a buyer at a large enterprise.

The fix

Put one piece of proof above the fold, next to the CTA.

Why it scores 6/10 · tap a row

’Hardcoded Secrets. Overprivileged Agents. Suspicious Skills.’ is punchy and specific jargon this buyer actually uses daily. Clear threat framing.
’Find Them All’ is a fun but vague button for a security assessment; the ad body’s ’Get a free 90-day assessment’ is clearer than the creative’s CTA.
Ad body text (’Overprivileged Agents Are Your Next Breach’) doesn’t fully match creative headline (’Hardcoded Secrets, Suspicious Skills’), two different threat lists running in parallel.
’Detect over-permissioned agents, embedded business data, and public exposure, automatically’ is concrete, but ’automatically’ and ’90-day assessment’ lack a quantified outcome or finding.
No named customer, logo, analyst mention or number anywhere in the ad; LinkedIn supports image-based trust signals and this creative skips them entirely.

Questions, answered

The ad is strong. The problem is the handoff: it offers a free 90-day Shadow AI assessment and the landing page only offers “Book a demo.”
The buyer clicked for one offer and is asked for a bigger commitment. A free assessment and a sales demo are different decisions, so the click has to be earned again.
Repeat the ad’s headline, make the assessment the only CTA, and add one piece of customer proof above the fold.
