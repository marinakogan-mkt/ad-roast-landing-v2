What’s happening

The ad does the hard part well: it sells a single product (OX.Cloud), backs it with an elite logo wall (IBM, Microsoft, Intel, Swisscom), and carries a concrete proof stat, 11,237 alerts reduced to 123 actionable ones. That is specific intent.

What’s happening

The click lands on a page selling four products and eight capabilities, under an H1, “Prompt to Run-time Security,” that reads like a typo and means nothing outside the category. The single-product promise dissolves into a platform overview.

The fix

Send the OX.Cloud click to an OX.Cloud page: the same product, the same stat, the same logos, so the specific intent the ad built gets a specific place to land.

Questions, answered

The ad is strong: it sells one product, OX.Cloud, with an elite logo wall and a real, specific proof stat (11,237 alerts down to 123). It scores 5 because of what happens after the click, not because of the ad itself.
On the landing page. A single-product ad lands on a page selling four products and eight capabilities, under a headline, “Prompt to Run-time Security,” that reads like a typo. The specific intent the ad created has nowhere specific to go.
Match the page to the product. Point the OX.Cloud click at an OX.Cloud page that keeps the same stat and the same logos, instead of a platform overview that restarts the sales conversation from zero.
