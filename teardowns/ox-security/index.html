Ad teardown · B2B SaaS

The ad promised a runtime incident.
The page offers a box.

OX Security’s Halloween ad has a line a security engineer feels: a runtime incident at 2 a.m., and how long until the team knows what happened. The button says “See a runtime incident.” The click lands on a Halloween page with a video to play and a box to claim, US only, while supplies last. Runtime incident response shows up later, third in a list of ten capabilities. Seasonal is fine. The problem is that the promise got left behind.

What the roast found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

“Process, network and MITRE tactic in one view” is concrete and credible to a security architect. It is the strongest thing in the creative.

What’s happening

“We’re sorry” and “the witching hour is real” come first. The 2 a.m. incident, the part that would stop the right reader, is smaller than the pumpkin.

The fix

Keep the Halloween wrapper light and let the 2 a.m. line be the biggest thing on the creative.

What’s happening

There is no customer, no number and no badge. A claim about seeing the full story needs someone who has seen it.

The fix

Add one number: time to understand an incident with OX Cloud, from a real customer.

What’s happening

The ad’s promise was specific: see a runtime incident. The top of the page is “Got you scared?” and a video. The reader has to scroll to find the word incident.

The fix

Open on the same 2 a.m. line, then show the incident view the button promised.

What’s happening

The first action on the page is a swag giveaway, US only, while supplies last. That collects addresses from people who clicked to see a product.

The fix

Make the incident walkthrough the first button and move the box below it.

What’s happening

The capability the ad sold sits in a list with AIDR, CSPM, KSPM, DSPM and six others. A one-product promise lands on a whole catalog.

The fix

Link the ad to the Runtime Events section, or lead the page with it.

Why it scores 6/10 · tap a row

’We’re sorry’ plus Halloween gags bury the actual hook (2 a.m. incident, full story in one view) under a seasonal gimmick.
’See a runtime incident’ is a soft, vague ask. For a mid-funnel capability pitch it should point clearer at a demo, not just ’learn more’.
Spiderwebs, pumpkins and ’witching hour’ compete with a serious SOC pain point; the Halloween skin dilutes rather than sharpens the incident-response message.
’Process, network and MITRE tactic in one view’ is concrete and credible to a security architect, the strongest line in the creative.
No customer name, number, or badge anywhere in the creative; LinkedIn supports logos and this ad uses none.

Questions, answered

The ad itself is decent: a real pain with a concrete capability, under a heavy costume. The damage happens after the click, where the promise of seeing an incident gives way to a video and a giveaway.
No. A seasonal wrapper can earn attention. It stops working when the costume replaces the promise, and the reader who clicked for a runtime incident can’t find one.
Open with the ad’s 2 a.m. line, make the incident walkthrough the first action, and keep the box as a bonus below it. Same campaign, and the promise survives.
