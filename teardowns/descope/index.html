What the roast found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

“Enterprise deals stall when identity gaps surface too late” names a specific, expensive moment. A VP of Engineering recognizes it instantly, with no jargon in the way.

What’s happening

The copy promises a score across “six foundational pillars” and stops there. SSO, SCIM and delegated admin are what the buyer is worried about, and the ad never says them.

The fix

Name two or three pillars in the ad. Specifics are what make a checklist feel worth a work email.

What’s happening

The creative is a staircase of checkmarks leading to a bank icon. It is clean and on-topic, and it carries no customer logo or number.

The fix

Add one customer logo or a line on who has already passed the checklist.

What’s happening

The ad and the page use the same title, the same six-pillar frame and the same asset. Nobody lands wondering if they are in the right place.

What’s happening

“A Practical Checklist for B2B Enterprise Readiness” describes the document. It leaves out what the reader walks away able to do.

The fix

Lead with the outcome: know which identity gaps will stall your next enterprise deal.

What’s happening

Four fields stand next to three bullets, with no logos, no download count and no preview of what is inside. For a gated asset, this is where confidence drops.

The fix

Put three or four customer logos beside the form and show one page of the checklist.

Why it scores 7/10 · tap a row

"Enterprise deals stall when identity gaps surface too late" names a real, specific pain that VPs of Eng recognize instantly. Clear, no jargon soup.
This is a content offer (checklist download), not a demo ask, so low friction is appropriate. No written CTA line in the ad copy itself is a minor gap.
Creative shows a rising staircase of checkmarks toward a bank icon, matching "score your readiness across six pillars." Clean but generic stock-style icon work.
"Six foundational pillars" is repeated everywhere but never named in the ad or creative. What are they? SSO, SCIM, MFA? Say it.
No customer logos or proof points on the creative image itself. LinkedIn supports logo badges here and this ad skips that lever entirely.

Questions, answered

The pain is right and the match is tight. The ad’s weakness is what it withholds: six pillars are promised and never named, and the creative carries no proof.
Pillars are what the reader is trading a work email for. Naming SSO, SCIM or delegated admin tells them the checklist covers their real problem, and it pre-qualifies the click.
Put proof next to the form: customer logos, a download count or a preview page. The form is the moment of doubt, so that is where the evidence belongs.
