What’s happening

A named customer (Rubrik), a named executive, and hard numbers: a 24-hour SLA and 187 compromised npm packages cleared in 30 minutes. This is the kind of proof most B2B ads never get to use.

What’s happening

The ad body turns the real figures into mush, “tight SLAs” instead of “24-hour SLA,” and leaves the operator quote that would stop the scroll completely unused. The strongest assets are in the story but not in the ad.

The fix

Lead with the numbers and the quote: 187 packages in 30 minutes, the named exec’s words, verbatim. Let the proof do the work it was built for.

Questions, answered

The material behind it is elite, a named customer, a named exec and real numbers. The verdict is “out of order” because the ad buries all of it: it turns hard figures into soft adjectives and leaves the best quote unused, so the strongest AppSec story of the quarter never actually shows up in the ad.
Sequencing. “24-hour SLA” becomes “tight SLAs,” “187 packages cleared in 30 minutes” goes missing, and the operator quote that would stop a scroll sits unused. The assets exist; they are just not the first thing the reader sees.
Put the proof first. Lead the ad with the hardest number (187 packages in 30 minutes) and the named exec’s quote, verbatim. The case study is strong enough to carry the ad if you stop softening it.
