What the roast found · tap to open each one

What’s happening

“See the shift” is a light CTA for a read, and that is the right size of ask for cold LinkedIn traffic.

What’s happening

“AI Changed Work. We Protect It.” is clean but vague. “What your DLP can’t see” is the sharper line, and it sits in the body copy.

The fix

Lead the creative with “What your DLP can’t see.”

What’s happening

“Your AI agents can now plan, act, and move data on their own” names the risk well. The ad never says what Cyberhaven does about it.

The fix

Add one line on what Cyberhaven sees that a DLP does not.

What’s happening

The ad has no named customer, stat or proof point. The most compelling number, a 60% adoption stat, only appears after the click.

The fix

Move that stat into the creative.

What’s happening

The post runs past 1,000 words before any product specificity. Cyberhaven Flow gets one passing mention, with no screenshot and no capability list.

The fix

Show what the product sees, early, for the reader the DLP hook attracted.

What’s happening

The menu has a demo button, but the article itself has no CTA, just share buttons and an AI summary tool. Paid traffic reads, agrees and leaves.

The fix

Add one next step tied to the hook, like seeing what your DLP misses.

Why it scores 6/10 · tap a row

’AI Changed Work. We Protect It.’ is clean but vague. ’What your DLP can’t see’ in the body copy is the sharper hook, buried.
’See the shift’ as a soft content CTA fits a blog-read offer, low friction, appropriately light ask for cold LinkedIn traffic.
Abstract star shapes and flowing lines look nice but say nothing about data, agents, or security; pure decoration over the strong headline.
’Your AI agents can now plan, act, and move data on their own’ names the risk well but never says what Cyberhaven actually does about it.
No named customer, stat, or proof point in the creative itself; the compelling 60% adoption stat lives on the landing page, not the ad.

Questions, answered
