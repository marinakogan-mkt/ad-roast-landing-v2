Webflow ads teardown: strong ads, a broken handoff

Webflow runs some genuinely sharp ads. One scores 8 out of 10, another 9. Then the click lands on a generic Contact Sales form and the specific promise disappears. The ad is not the problem. The handoff is. Here is the 40-second teardown, and the written version below it.

AdRoast teardown of Webflow's live LinkedIn and Google ads. 37 seconds, sound on.

Most ad feedback stops at the creative. This one follows the click. We pulled Webflow's live ads from the LinkedIn Ad Library and the Google Ads Transparency Center, scored each one against the buyer it was built for, and then opened the page behind the best of them. The ads are not the issue. What happens after the click is.

Act 1: the weak ones. Enterprise product, freelancer language.

A chunk of Webflow's Google search ads lead with "Powerful Website CMS & Hosting," "Try Now For Free," and "Visually create custom blogs, portfolios, eCommerce sites." That is SMB and freelancer language for a product that, at the top of the funnel, is trying to win enterprise buyers. The enterprise context is gone before the click. A buyer evaluating a composable CMS for a cross-functional team does not see themselves in "blogs and portfolios," so the ad quietly pulls in the wrong person.

Act 2: the strong ones. Same account, close to buyer pain.

8

"Design Meets CMS — Skip the Front-End Rebuild." Names the rebuild problem directly, the exact pain an enterprise web team feels. Scored 8 out of 10. This is what a good ad looks like: a specific, recognizable problem in the headline.

9

"Arkose Labs rebuilt a decade-old site in 3.5 weeks." (LinkedIn) A named customer plus a number that holds up. Scored 9 out of 10. Proof a buyer can picture beats any adjective. "Webflow Enterprise, more than a website builder" also lands, speaking to three buyer roles and a composable CMS.

Act 3: the handoff. Two promises, one generic form.

Here is where the money leaks. One promoted ad makes two specific promises: run experiments and "personalize for every visitor, and let AI deliver the winning experience," plus "structured content, built for developers and marketers." Strong, specific, buyer-aware. Then the click lands on webflow.com/enterprise/contact-sales: a generic "Contact Webflow Sales team" form asking for work email and company.

AI personalization? Not on the page. Structured content? Not on the page. It is a shared form with no message continuation, and it asks for a meeting before the case is finished. The pain the ad named never shows up. A visitor who clicked because of a specific promise arrives at a page that makes a different, generic ask, and most of them leave.

Webflow's best ad scores 9 out of 10. It still loses the buyer, because the page behind it drops the promise. That is not an ad quality problem. It is a handoff problem.
Marina Kogan

The lesson

When your ads are already good and the pipeline still will not move, stop polishing the creative. Take your highest-spending ad, read the exact promise it makes, then open its landing page and read only the first screen. If the promise is not repeated there, in roughly the same words, you have found the leak. The fix is almost never a new ad. It is a page that keeps the promise the ad made.
