Best LinkedIn ad audit tools in 2026

"Audit my LinkedIn ads" means four different jobs: is the ad talking to the right buyer, does the message land, which accounts did it reach, and what happens after the click. No tool does all four well. Here is what each one actually checks, so you pick by the question you have, not by the logo.

Disclosure: I built AdRoast.in, and it is on this list. I put it where I think it fits and say what it does not do. Everything else here is a tool I would point a client to for the job described. Nobody paid to be included.

Buyer fit and the click

1. AdRoast.in

Best for: B2B SaaS teams that want every live ad scored against their actual buyer. Free first audit, then $29 or $79 a month.

You paste your website URL. AdRoast pulls your live LinkedIn and Google ads, infers your ideal buyer from your site, scores each ad 0 to 10 on whether that buyer would feel seen, and follows each click to check the landing page repeats the promise. The output is a ranked board with the exact rewrite for the weakest ads. You can see public examples on the live ad boards and teardowns.

What it does not do: it does not read your Campaign Manager, so it has no spend, CTR, or CPL. It judges the message, not the bidding. Pair it with one of the attribution tools below if you need the numbers.

2. LinkedIn Ad Library

Best for: seeing exactly what is live, yours or a competitor's. Free.

LinkedIn's own public library of ads. Search a company and you get the creatives running now. It is step one of any audit, because teams are almost always surprised by what is still spending.

What it does not do: no scoring, no landing pages, no grouping. You get a scroll of ads and have to judge them yourself.

Message testing

3. Wynter

Best for: testing a message or page with real B2B buyers before you put budget behind it.

Wynter runs your copy past a panel of verified B2B professionals in your target role and returns what they understood, what confused them, and what they did not believe, usually within a day or two. It answers a question no ad-data tool can: how the buyer reads the words.

What it does not do: it tests what you give it. It will not pull your live ads or tell you which of the forty in your account are the problem. Panel coverage is strongest for SaaS roles.

Account reach and attribution

4. DemandSense

Best for: LinkedIn advertisers who want to know which companies they reached and where spend is wasted.

A LinkedIn Ads attribution and optimization platform listed as a LinkedIn marketing partner. It ties ad impressions and site visits to CRM accounts and adds controls like frequency capping and budget re-allocation.

What it does not do: it measures delivery and engagement, not whether the message fits the buyer. An ad can reach the right account and still say the wrong thing.

5. ZenABM

Best for: ABM teams whose motion runs mostly on LinkedIn Ads.

Pulls LinkedIn Ads data next to your CRM to show which target accounts engaged with your ads and how that connects to pipeline. It has expanded to other channels and pushes company-level engagement into HubSpot.

What it does not do: same gap as DemandSense. Great at "who saw it", silent on "was it the right thing to say".

After the click

6. Microsoft Clarity

Best for: watching what LinkedIn traffic does on the landing page. Free.

Session recordings and heatmaps. Filter to visits from your LinkedIn UTMs and watch where people stop scrolling, rage-click, or leave. If an ad gets clicks and the page gets bounces, this shows you where.

What it does not do: it shows behavior, not the reason. It will not tell you the page changed the subject from what the ad promised; you have to spot that.

7. A manual ICP-fit audit

Best for: doing it once by hand so you know what to look for. Free, about 45 minutes.

Pull your live ads, write your buyer in one sentence, score each ad 0 to 10 on recognition, follow every click, and cut the bottom of the list. The full method is in How to audit your LinkedIn ads against your ICP.

How to choose

Start with the symptom. Clicks but no pipeline is almost always a buyer-fit or landing-page problem, so start with AdRoast.in or the manual audit, then Clarity on the page (more in why B2B ads leak after the click). Good leads but you do not know which accounts is an attribution problem: DemandSense or ZenABM. New positioning you have not shipped yet is a message-testing job: Wynter. Most teams need two of these, one for the message and one for the numbers.
