CheckThe question

Problem and outcomeDoes it name a problem they already have and what changes after?

How it works

1

Paste a website. Yours or a competitor's. There is nothing to connect, because the ads come from the public ad libraries.

2

AdRoast finds the buyer. It reads the site and writes down who the company sells to. Every score is measured against that buyer, not against generic best practice.

3

Every live ad gets a score. You get a board of real creatives, ranked 0 to 10, with the weakest ones easy to spot.

4

You get the fix. Open an ad for the full report: what breaks, whether the landing page holds up, and the rewrite. A free account opens your first report, no card.

What low-scoring ads have in common

1,012 of the scored ads came in at 5 or below. The most common complaint in their scoring notes, in roughly 3 of every 10, is generic language: a headline any company in the category could run. The other patterns repeat too, and they often stack in the same ad. These are real notes from the boards.

1

It describes the category, not the difference."Category-label headline with a generic benefit line, no differentiator or proof for the ICP buyer."

2

It reaches the right buyer and promises nothing."Speaks to the right buyer but offers no outcome beyond 'consistency', and no proof."

3

The claim has nothing behind it."Named customer and CIO angle are credible, but no outcome number makes the story too thin to compel a click."

4

It repeats the tagline instead of the pain."Headline mirrors the brand tagline with no buyer pain or outcome, and 'agentic ai' adds jargon without proof."

5

It never says why to click today."Positioning is clear but the message stays at category level with no outcome, proof, or reason to click now."

What this audit does not do

It does not read Campaign Manager, so it has no spend, CTR, or CPL, and it cannot tell you which ad made money. It judges the message, not the bidding. It also needs live ads in the public libraries: a company that is not running any has nothing to score. Questions

Is it really free? The first audit is free and needs no card. After that, Starter is $29 a month for one company and 25 roasts, and Pro is $79 a month for up to five companies and 100 roasts.

Do I need to connect Campaign Manager? No. AdRoast reads the public ad libraries, so there is nothing to connect and no account access to grant.

What is a good score? Across 5,489 scored ads, the average company sits at about 6.6 out of 10. About 1 in 4 ads scores 8 or higher, and about 1 in 5 scores 5 or lower.

Can I audit a competitor? Yes. The ads are public, so the audit works the same on any company's website.
